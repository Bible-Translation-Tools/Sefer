## 2. Sous — a real character census, not only convictions

**Lower priority: an idea for now** (Will, 2026-10-20). Kept whole for when it comes back.

**Asked:** a character-by-character inventory page (occurrences, spread, neighbours, placement, clusters) with "show the other places this character appears".

**State:** the Sous publication's pattern table holds a row only when a channel has a claim to make. On `fixtures/small-nt` that is 11 rows over 10 glyphs, all convicted, and zero word patterns. `/inventory` therefore labels itself "the characters the engine measured". Will: "current sous doesn't have the full detail; I'll change there."

**Change, per glyph in the corpus (convicted or not):**

- total sites and per-book spread
- the full neighbour table on both sides (exact and pooled)
- placement against Letter / Space / Digit / Nonletter / Edge
- run (cluster) shapes
- **an API that enumerates every site of a glyph or of a pattern**, not only the convicted ones

Rarity's denominator today is the corpus total, so `Glyph.sites` has to take Rarity's numerator and the other channels' denominator. A per-glyph total would remove that special case.

**Sefer side ready:**

- `src/core/findings/inventory.ts`, `Finding.pattern`, `/inventory` ([inventory](../../documentation/architecture/inventory.md))
- `sitesOfGlyph` is parked ([parked](/items/2026-09-23-parked)) until a caller wants it

## 11. `OnionBook::locate` walks the whole mask on every call

**Asked 2026-10-15**, for the WACS hub; found 2026-10-14 by profiling the hub's
proofreading of `wa-catalog/hbo_uhb` with samply. The code is unchanged in
v0.1.10: `galley/src/sous/onion_book.rs`, `locate` and `SourceSpans`.

There are two problems:

- `locate` starts its `SourceSpans` at mask range 0, not at the first range
  that can overlap.
- `next()` never stops early: after the last overlapping range it scans to the
  end of `mask.ranges`.

So draining one `locate` costs O(mask ranges), and a host that locates every
finding pays O(findings × ranges).

On hbo_uhb that was 90% of a 4 s proofread, with 163,284 findings; the analysis
itself took about 0.2 s. The hub now keeps its own copy of the mask
(`OnionBook::from_parts`, `Mask::to_source`), and the output is byte-identical
on en_ulb, hbo_uhb, km_ulb and ru_ulb with its source. hbo_uhb then proofreads
in 237 ms.

**Change.** Any one of these fixes it for every caller:

1. **Start where the range starts, and stop where it ends.** Begin at
   `mask.starts.partition_point(|&s| s <= range.from()) - 1`, and return `None`
   once a projected start is at or past `range.to()`. This is a pure fix: the
   cost becomes O(log ranges + runs).
2. **`raw_bounds(range) -> Option<Range<u32>>`.** It answers what most callers
   ask: from `to_source(from)` to `to_source(to - 1) + 1`.
3. **`pub fn mask(&self) -> &Mask`**, so a host need not rebuild the mask beside
   the book.

**Check.** A book of thousands of short verses: locate a range near the end and
drain `spans`. Assert the same runs as today, and that `next()` touches only the
ranges it returns plus one. Or time the hub's
`cargo run --release -p hub-proof --example proofread_repo -- <hbo_uhb.git>`
before and after.

**Sefer side:** none. Galley's own paths (Sous siting and pairing, overlay)
build an `OnionBook` but map through the mask directly and never call
`locate`, so Sefer's in-browser proofreading does not pay this. The hub drops
its mask copy (`parse_book` in `crates/proof/src/lib.rs`) once (1), (2) or (3)
ships.

## 12. Tile the TOC so a unit's opening markers belong to it

**Not yet asked.** A verse that opens a paragraph starts its unit at `\v`, so the `\s5` and `\p`
before it trail the PREVIOUS unit (Mark 14:6). A card is its TOC unit and does not widen
itself, so Backspace at such a card's first verse cannot reach the paragraph break that
visibly opens it.

**Change:** tile backward from each unit's start: a `\p` (and like openers: `\q`, `\m`, a
section heading) attaches forward to the unit it opens, not back as trailing text.

**Sefer side:** cards and Review's units include their opening paragraph break; nothing else.

## 13. A long run of one letter is not flagged

**Not yet asked.** Will, 2026-10-14: `sssssssssssssssssssssssere` does not raise a finding;
only a run of exactly three does. A run longer than three is at least as suspect.

**Change:** flag a run of three OR MORE of one letter (or say which rule decides, if the
exactly-three is deliberate).

**Check:** a verse with runs of 3, 4 and 26 of one letter; each is a finding.

## 14. `resolve`: a reference to its place in one text

**Not yet asked.** `resolve` answers "where is JHN 3:16 in this text": given a book's table of
contents and an Address, found, missing or ambiguous. The hard cases make it more than a lookup:

- a bridge: asking for verse 2 when the file has `\v 1-2`;
- a list with a gap: `\v 1,3` has no verse 2;
- segments: `3a` and `3b`;
- verses missing from the file;
- ambiguity: two `\v 3` in one chapter.

It lives only in Sefer: `packages/text/src/location/locate.ts`, 489 lines over the engine's
TOC, with careful rules and no tests. Kitchen has only the reverse, from a span to a verse id.

**Why the engine:** anyone who calls `resolve` already needs the engine's TOC, so a port to
another language gains nothing and adds a copy whose subtle rules drift. Sefer's rules become the
spec.

**Change:** a `resolve(toc, address)` door returning found (with the span, and whether it is
coarser than asked: a bridge or a whole verse for a segment), missing, or ambiguous (every
candidate).

**Tests first, either way:** a USFM snippet plus a reference, and the expected answer, one per
case above. Sefer can write them against `locate.ts` today, and they become the engine's
acceptance tests.

**Sefer side:** call the door; `locate.ts` keeps only the types, or goes.

## 15. A stable identity for an onion diagnostic

**Not yet asked.** `sous-identity` (v0.1.10) names a Sous finding so a suppression survives
edits elsewhere and settings changes: `v1:s` / `v1:f`, the verse, the flagged text, its ordinal.
An onion lint finding has nothing like it, so Sefer cannot save "this is fine" for one without
inventing its own format.

**Change:** in the same module and contract, by its `DiagnosticName`:

- a site identity for a diagnostic, `v1:o:<DiagnosticName>:<verse>:<flagged text>:<ordinal>`,
  escaped as the others are ("fine here");
- a subject identity where a diagnostic has one, e.g. an unknown marker by its name
  ("always allow `\zqq`" without turning off unknown-marker findings), as `v1:p` is for Sous.

Pinned on the golden fixtures like the Sous identities.

**Sefer side:** `.sefer/onion-suppressions.json` keyed by it
([project state](/items/2026-10-06-project-state-and-suppressions)).

Sefer's own, not kitchen's (Will, 2026-10-20; was kitchen ask 4):

> I don't think it's terribly complex, but it will be a new module of like, local lint maybe?
> cause want to draw diagnostics where it's not consistent in a book or across books. I would
> say this is a rule is the thing.

- **A rule:** find every `\cl` per book, reduce, and report where labels are inconsistent in a
  book or across books. Drawn with the engine's findings through the host-diagnostics door
  (`CorpusFinding`, `setHostDiagnostics`).
- **A fix:** rewrite to one shape, e.g. per-chapter `\cl Psalm 1`, `\cl Psalm 2` to one
  book-level `\cl Psalm` before `\c 1`, or back. An edit through the Book, offered by the
  finding (the fix lives in the editor package's `core/fixes`).
- **A workflow** visits each in order, or shows them grouped and replaces in one pass.

## The plan (2026-10-22)

Scripture Kitchen agrees it is Sefer's, and that it is its own small pass over the parse
tree, not part of onion's lint or of Sous:

> Lint checks one file against the USFM spec, and both \cl forms are valid USFM.
> "Inconsistent" is a project convention, and it compares books, which lint never sees.
> [...] Sous finds patterns statistically, from counts. This is a fixed rule over a handful
> of facts per book, with an exact fix.

What it needs from kitchen: nothing. onion's tables already tell the two meanings apart
(`BookChapterLabel` before the first `\c`, `ChapterContent` after one), and every `\cl`
paragraph is in the tree with its position.

The USFM usage note, for the two meanings:

> If cl is added to a text once before chapter 1 it represents the label text for "Chapter"
> to be used throughout the current book. If cl is used after each individual chapter, it
> represents the specific label text to be used for the display of the current chapter
> (e.g. if numbers are being presented as words, not numerals).

Will's concern was the lifecycle:

> the MAIN thing about all this if we do it here is that we need to hook is that's not exactly
> paint and parse, but we do need the cst (a pub sub? or not sure), and it's a new module/layer
> of app level consistency type checks [...] But joins the lifecycle of diagnostics and the
> diagnostic sinks.

### The tree is already held: no new subscription

ProjectAnalysis parses every book when a project opens and keeps each book's `Analysis`,
whose `dish.tree` has every `\cl`. It already re-analyzes a changed book and publishes once,
debounced (~150 ms quiet), and the open book's parse is handed in by the editor, never parsed
twice. The rule is one more step in that pass ([findings](../../documentation/architecture/findings.md),
"What ProjectAnalysis holds"). That also answers kitchen's one condition for building a
`chapterLabels(id)` read on galley ("if parsing every book just to find a few \cl lines turns
out too slow"): we parse every book anyway.

```ts
type Label = { scope: "book" | "chapter"; chapter: number | null; text: string; from: number; to: number };
type Style = "book-word" | "per-chapter" | "mixed" | "none";

labelsOf(analysis): Label[]                 // per book, cached by the book's sourceHash
styleOf(labels): Style
judge(byBook: Map<bookId, Label[]>): Finding[]   // the cross-book reduction, info severity
```

### It joins the sinks the way Sous does

A Sous finding is already a whole-project judgement delivered after a publish, so this rides
the same path rather than a new one:

- **The one shape.** `Finding` with producer `sefer` and codes `sefer.chapterLabel.mixed`,
  `sefer.chapterLabel.wordDiffers`, `sefer.chapterLabel.bookDiffers`; each stamped with its
  book's `SourceStamp` and `EngineStamp`, so `stale` and the fix's check work unchanged.
- **Inline (sink 1b).** `crossBookOf(bookId)` already feeds the editor's
  `setHostDiagnostics`; the rule's share joins that list, and any document change empties it
  until the next publish, as Sous's does.
- **Panel and badges (sinks 2, 3).** Through `findings()` and `findingTotals()`.
- **Telemetry (sink 4).** Counts and codes only.
- **Suppressions.** "This is fine" works on them as on any finding.

The new thing is the folder: `src/core/rules/` is where later project-wide consistency checks
go, each a pure function from the held analyses to findings.

### What is flagged

- **In a book:** per-chapter labels whose word differs ("Psalm 3" beside "Psalms 4"); a
  book-level word with per-chapter labels that are "word + number" in a different word.
- **Across books:** a book whose style differs from what most books use ("one book differs").
- **Not flagged:** a book with no `\cl` (most projects have none, and flagging absence lights
  up every project); a per-chapter label that is not "word + number" ("Chapter One",
  "Psalm Twenty-Three"), which is the usage note's second meaning and a legitimate choice.

Info severity: it is mostly a print concern, it only matters once someone typesets, and
surfacing it early costs translators nothing.

### The fix: a second kind

`core/fixes` knows only engine fixes and refuses anything else (`NotEngineFix`). This adds a
second kind: the rule computes its edits from the current analysis at the moment of the
click, refuses if the engine stamp moved, and applies through the Book, so Undo, Save and
Recovery hear it as they hear every other edit.

Offered only when it is safe, as kitchen says:

> Turning per-chapter labels into one book-level label is only safe when every label is
> "the same word + the chapter number" (Psalm 1, Psalm 2). Labels written as words
> (Chapter One) are the usage note's second meaning and can't collapse to a book-level word.

Both directions: per-chapter to one `\cl Psalm` before `\c 1`, and back.

### UI: marks to notice it, a dialog to fix it

- **Inline:** an info squiggle on each `\cl` that differs. Its popover says why ("this book
  labels each chapter; most books use one word for the whole book") and has one action,
  **Review chapter labels…**.
- **The dialog:** one row per book: its style, a sample ("Psalm", "Psalm 3", "Chapter One"),
  whether it matches the majority, and **Fix** on each row where the fix is safe. At the top,
  **Make all like the majority**, which fixes every safe book in one pass. A dialog because
  this is a rare, whole-project chore; a visit-each-in-order workflow is not worth it for a
  handful of lines per book.

## Built (2026-10-29, branch editor/keymaps-chapter-labels)

Will's rulings that day changed the shape: the dialog opens from a chapter's title, not from a finding, and it works per book.

- **The editor** names a chapter from any `\cl` inside the chapter's span (the parse's chapter rows), never "the line after `\c`". Only the `\c` line and the label lines are hidden; the title is drawn on the `\c` line, so a `\s` between them stays visible and reachable. The label lines take edits only from the dialog (`project.chapterLabels` in `HEADING_WRITERS`).
- **The rule** is `src/core/rules/chapterLabels.ts`: every `\cl` off the parse's tokens, shaped `word-number`, `number-word` or `other` against its chapter's number; `standardize(book, word)` writes or rewrites one book label before `\c 1` and takes out the chapter labels that are that word and their number. Labels written as words and stray numbers are kept; a book whose numbers come first ("3 Sura") has its words rewritten in place instead.
- **The dialog** (`src/app/ui/rules/ChapterLabels.tsx`): one row per book with labels, its words with counts (most used first), "Another word", "Leave as it is", and a "Use for every book" box at the top. Labels kept as written are listed under their book. Standardize is one cross-book edit (`chapterLabels.standardize`), one Undo.

**Findings (built 2026-10-29, Will's rulings):** every chapter label of the form word + number whose word is not its book's is flagged, info severity; a book with a book label has declared its word and is judged against it alone, a book without one against the project's most used word; a number that is not its chapter's is flagged too; labels written as words never are. One entry in the Findings list for them all ("N chapter labels read differently"; its Fine here turns the check off); a squiggle on each label (on the drawn title in Reading and Form Mode), whose popup offers Review chapter labels…, Fine here, and "“word” is fine everywhere", which silences that word in every book. Producer `project`, the first user of it.
